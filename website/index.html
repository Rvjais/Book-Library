SQL was developed in the early 1970s at IBM’s San Jose research facilities. It is a declarative language, meaning that queries describe what data is needed rather than how to navigate disk storage.

As relational engines grew in power and sophistication, database engineers discovered that separating data storage abstractions from high-level queries allowed for dramatic optimizations: b-tree indexing, query caching, and dynamic execution plans.

Privacy Guarantee

Built offline from line zero

Most reading apps require an account, upload your reading speed to remote servers, and track every bookmark you make. LibReady rejects all of that.

No Network Permissions

The Android release APK declares no internet permission in its manifest. The operating system prevents the app from accessing external networks.

Isolated App Sandbox

All imported files, generated cover art, reading offsets, and notes are stored strictly inside the device’s sandboxed Documents folder.

No Accounts or Logins

No usernames, passwords, or emails. Your identity is a local cryptographic key pair stored solely on your hardware.

True Clean Deletion

When you delete a book, its files, cover thumbnails, reading progress, and bookmark database records are permanently erased.

Offline Certified

Verified Android Manifest contains zero network permission declarations.
